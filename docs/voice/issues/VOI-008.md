# VOI-008: Live Speech-to-Text Subtitles

# What

- **Live subtitles for speech** - Display real-time speech-to-text transcription
  on screen as the person speaks during an active voice session.
- **Cinematic movie styling** - Style the subtitles like classic movie subtitles
  (clean high-contrast typography, crisp off-white text with a subtle dark outline
  or translucent backdrop, centered near the bottom of the viewport for maximum
  legibility over any active channel).
- **Ephemeral feedback** - Words pop up dynamically as speech occurs and dismiss
  gracefully when the utterance completes, the turn yields to thinking/speaking,
  or the session ends.
- **Private by design** - Transient subtitle text exists strictly in browser
  memory for active visual feedback; no transcripts are written to durable logs,
  metrics, or storage.

Before:

- The voice interaction UI only shows abstract state labels ("Listening",
  "Hearing you", "Thinking", "Speaking") and an audio-level progress bar.
- Spoken words are never visible on screen as the person speaks, offering no
  immediate visual feedback of what was recognized.

After:

- When a person speaks during an active voice session, recognized words appear
  live on screen in a movie-subtitle format.
- Subtitle text updates progressively in real time as more speech is recognized.
- Subtitles disappear cleanly as soon as the utterance finishes and the assistant
  begins thinking or responding, or if the session is cancelled or ends.

## Out Of Scope

- Assistant response text subtitles or closed captions for synthesized audio.
- Transcript export, saving, or conversation history logging.
- Custom subtitle font/color settings or complex configuration toggles.
- Passive capture or subtitles outside an explicitly activated voice session.

# Heatmap

### H1 - Subtitle visual presentation and placement

- Decision: Subtitle positioning, typography, and contrast treatment.
- Proposed approach: Position subtitles centered horizontally near the bottom of
  the screen (above the voice capture bar), using bold, clean sans-serif
  typography with subtle text shadows and a translucent dark backing pill so text
  is clearly readable over bright or dark channels (Today, Music, Camera, etc.).
- Why: Matches the familiar movie subtitle aesthetic while keeping channel
  content and status indicators uncluttered.
- Alternatives: Full-width caption overlay, top-anchored banner, or inline text
  inside the voice capture pill.
- Review focus: Ensure subtitles remain legible across high-contrast background
  changes (e.g. camera view, album artwork) without covering key controls.

### H2 - Real-time streaming transcription pipeline

- Decision: Mechanism for relaying partial speech recognition to the browser.
- Proposed approach: Stream incremental Vosk recognition results (partial
  transcripts) from the coordinator or speech recognizer to the client during
  active speech.
- Why: Provides instantaneous visual feedback as words are spoken rather than a
  delayed single block of text after speech has ended.
- Alternatives: Post-utterance final transcript display only (lacks live "as I
  speak" feedback), or client-side Web Speech API (inconsistent across browsers
  and violates local speech engine boundaries).
- Review focus: Low latency between spoken audio and on-screen word updates.

### H3 - Ephemeral lifecycle and privacy boundaries

- Decision: State lifecycle for subtitle text in the frontend.
- Proposed approach: Subtitle text is held purely in ephemeral component state,
  cleared immediately on turn completion, cancellation, or session teardown,
  and never dispatched into durable telemetry, diagnostics, or error logs.
- Why: Conforms to the project privacy rule: no spoken content persisted or logged.
- Alternatives: Retaining transcript history in memory across multiple turns.
- Review focus: Verify that cancelling a turn or closing the session leaves zero
  residual transcript text in the DOM or state stores.

# How to test

### Live subtitle rendering during speech

- **Start** - Run `./coordinator/develop` on the IdeaPad and open
  `http://127.0.0.1:5173`.
- **Action** - Start a voice session with `Ctrl`+`Alt`+`Space` and speak a
  sentence naturally (e.g. "What is the weather today?").
- **Expected** - As words are spoken, subtitles pop up near the bottom of the
  screen in movie-subtitle style, updating progressively with the utterance.

### Subtitle legibility across channels

- **Start** - Navigate between Today (`Ctrl`+`Alt`+`1`), Music
  (`Ctrl`+`Alt`+`2`), and Camera (`Ctrl`+`Alt`+`3`).
- **Action** - Speak phrases while viewing each channel.
- **Expected** - Subtitles are clearly readable against light, dark, and live
  video backgrounds without layout clipping or collision with the channel UI.

### Ephemeral cleanup on turn completion and cancellation

- **Start** - Start a voice session and begin speaking.
- **Action** - Finish the sentence and observe the transition to thinking and
  speaking. Then start speaking again and press `Escape` mid-utterance.
- **Expected** - Subtitles dismiss cleanly when speech stops and the turn
  transitions to assistant thinking/speaking. Pressing `Escape` immediately
  clears the subtitle from screen. No transcript text remains in diagnostics
  (`Ctrl`+`Alt`+`D`), browser logs, or storage.
